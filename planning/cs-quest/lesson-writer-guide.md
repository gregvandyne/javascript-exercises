# CS Quest · lesson writer guide (curriculum owner A, stage 1 → stages 2-4)

You are writing one of the 46 lesson files of `seed-lessons/cs-quest/lessons/` in the worktree
`/home/user/javascript-exercises/.worktrees/curriculum` (branch `cs-quest/curriculum`). Everything a lesson needs is in three
places: the exemplar `seed-lessons/cs-quest/lessons/1.2-1b.json` (copy it), the schema in `seed-lessons/cs-quest/README.md`,
and the plan prose for your lesson in `plan.md` (line numbers in the table below). The machine-readable truth for ids, quest
codes, params, warm-up items and step shapes is `contracts.json` in this folder (`lessons[]`, regenerated by
`gen-contracts.py` with the critic additions baked in); `units.json` and `games.json` in the repo are generated from it.

Run after every lesson: `node --test tests/unit/cs-quest-catalogue.test.js` (3 s). It validates the schema, the platform's
limits, the quest code and params against the chapter table, the warm-up and exit items against owner B's banks (the
ignored local copies under `seed-lessons/cs-quest/sets/`), US spelling and the real game scanner over every string. The
coverage clauses switch on by themselves once all 46 files exist.

## 1. File naming and identity

- `seed-lessons/cs-quest/lessons/<unit>.<n>-<1b|2>.json`: `1.2-1b.json` (Explorer, grades 3-5, CSTA level 1B),
  `1.2-2.json` (Pro, grades 6-8, level 2). One file per band; both bands share `n`, `order`, `name`, `short`.
- `id` = `key` = `csq-<1b|2>-<u.n>`; `version: 1`; `band` `1B` | `2`; `bandCode` `1b` | `2`; `grade` `Grades 3-5` | `Grades 6-8`.
- `title` = `CS Quest <u.n> · <name>` (the library title; <= 100 chars). `name` and `short` come from `units.json`
  (`short` is the bank's `topic`, the Brain Board column, the derived warm-up set's name: `CSQ 1.2 warm-up · Loops (3-5)`).
- Copy `csta.primary / also / buildsToward` exactly from `units.json` (= the table below). Never move an id between lists.

## 3. The steps (student-visible; kids read these)

Default: `Today` text 17 · `Warm-up (live)` game 5 · `Play: <Chapter title>` game 18 · `Exit ticket` quiz 3 · `In your own words`
question 2 = 45. Variants are in the table (6.1: Today 27, no warm-up, WYR 13; 6.2 Pro: Play A 10 + Play B 8; 7.1: three
question steps 6+6+6; 7.2: `Builder etiquette` text 5 replaces the warm-up, `Build your game` build 18; 7.3: `Playtest
protocol` text 3 + `Tally and the one change` question 15; 7.4: `Credits and How-to` question 18, Pro `Credits, How-to and
How it works`). Copy the step objects of the exemplar and change only the content:

- `Today` body: `Do Now: ...` ¶ `Big idea: ...` ¶ `We will talk about:` + the discuss prompts as `- ` lines. Plain text;
  blank line between paragraphs. No program name, no codes, no teacher-only words.
- `Warm-up (live)`: `gameSlug` `quest-race` (1B) / `question-battle` (2), `live: "warmup"`, `playHidden: true`, no options,
  no standards (the live run writes the score). Short body.
- `Play: ...`: `gameSlug`, `options` (= `params`), `questCode`, `standards` (from the table: CA-2 moved some ids to the
  question step in 2.2, 3.3 Explorer, 6.2 Explorer), body: goal, stretch, `If the game asks for a quest code, it is 2 1 0 0.`,
  pairs rule. The test checks the body contains the quest code and that standards are carried by the chapter (or its
  `then` chapter) for the band.
- `Would You Rather (live)` (6.1, 6.3): `gameSlug: "would-you-rather-live"`, `live: "wyr"`, `starter`, `playHidden: true`.
- Unit-7 question / build steps: `standards` and `rubric` exactly as the table says (the capstone rubric lines, plan.md
  1269-1279: `{ line: "Plan", ids: [...], levels: [4] }`; the `Program` line on the 7.2 build step has `ids: []` because it
  counts as evidence for nothing). Build step: `template: "quiz"`, body = the message placeholder first (`Paste your team's
  Builder message here (up to 300 characters).`) then the etiquette card.
- `Exit ticket`: `set: "exit"`, minutes 3.
- `In your own words`: `standards` (the table's "In your own words" ids), `rubric: "exit"`, body = `exit.sentencePrompt` +
  `Frame: <exit.frame>` (the test checks the frame is in the body).

Every primary id must appear in some step's `standards` (Practiced) and in some rubric line (Applied): `exit.rubric` has
one line per primary id, so the second half is automatic.

## 4. Voice rules (teacher-facing prose; plain, short, imperative)

- The plan page prints `Say:`, `Do:`, `Ask:` labels itself. `explore.say[]` holds the exact words to say (sentences a
  teacher reads aloud, no quotation marks, no stage directions). `explore.do[]` are imperative instructions to the teacher
  (`Open ... Run it. Delete five ...`). `explore.ask[]` are the questions as asked; `afterAsk` is what to do with the answer.
  Two Say, one or two Do, one Ask, one big idea (one sentence a kid can repeat), <= 5 vocabulary words (6 only in 5.1).
- `doNow` is paper-answerable and board-sized. `discuss[]` = two prompts: turn-and-talk, then whole class; `showMe` names
  the projector moment. `lookFors[]` say what you will see, `misconception` is one sentence that names the wrong idea and
  the right one. `differentiation.support / stretch` are concrete moves, not adjectives. `subPlan` is three sentences a
  substitute can run with no projector and no login (quest code included, "signed out is fine").
- Kid-facing text (step titles and bodies): second person, short lines, nothing a kid should not read (no "CS Quest", no
  `1B-AP-10`, no "the teacher will mark"). Teacher-facing text may say both.
- Names: Bolt (Robo Route), Dot (Data Detective), Pim (Packet Post), Gizmo (Inside the Machine); chapter titles as in
  games.json (`Loops`, `Debug Lab`, `Break It Up`, `Fix-It Desk`, ...). Bands are "Explorer" / "Pro" in the game and
  "grades 3-5" / "grades 6-8" on the page. Say "trusted adult" in lesson text (the games keep "grown-up"), "many
  computers and cables" not "the cloud", no brands, no product names, no real people, no site names, no URLs.
- US spelling (the test fails British forms): color, gray, favorite, labeled, practiced, neighbor, recognize, math,
  center, program, catalog, organize, license, analyze, modeling, canceled, meters, artifact. "toward", not "towards".
- Numbers: never 7+ digits in a row; quest codes with spaces (`2 1 0 0`); RGB as `255, 0, 128`; no `@`, no `//`, no `http`.
  Never put "code" within 40 characters of grown-up / parent / guardian / family / mom / dad (the game scanner reads that
  as a family link-code prompt); never "type your password" (4.3's Do Now message says `Reply with your mailbox key`).
- Materials list the lesson's additions (printables by sheet key, a chime, paper slips); the program list (projector,
  devices, headphones, login cards) lives in `units.json`.

## 5. Per-band vocabulary and the 40-minute cut

The Explorer file carries the 1B words; the Pro file carries them plus the Pro additions (still <= 5 on the learn card;
pick the five the Explore script actually uses and put the rest in `notes`). `shortPeriod40`: Do Now 4 · projector
warm-up with 2 items 3 (the first retrieval item and the vocabulary item) · Explore 5 · Play 16 · one Discuss prompt 6 ·
exit ticket + pack up 6 = 40. 6.1's cut: Do Now 4 · Timeline Walk 8 · WYR 13 · exit 5 ... make it sum to 40 and keep the
exit ticket. 7.2's period is 5 · 4 · 30 · 1 · 5 (Discuss is one minute by construction); its cut takes 5 from the build block.

## 8. Checklist before you commit a lesson

1. `node --test tests/unit/cs-quest-catalogue.test.js` is green (your file's four or five subtests pass).
2. The Explore script works on the projector with level 1 of the locked chapter (or the `exploreDemo` level for 4.1 Pro).
3. Exit sentence frame and `exit.rubric` descriptors match the three bank items of the lesson (plan.md 913-1188).
4. `shortPeriod40` sums to 40 and keeps the exit ticket; `plan` sums to 45; steps sum to 45.
5. Nothing a kid reads says the program name or a standard code; nothing anywhere names a brand, a person or a price.
6. `git add seed-lessons/cs-quest/lessons/<file>` explicitly (never `git add -A`; `sets/` and `wyr-tech.json` are owner B's and
   must never be committed here); commit with the two trailer lines; push at the end of the stage.
